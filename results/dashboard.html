8. Honest Engineering Analysis: Limitations & Physical Trade-Offs

In rigorous networking research, domain-specific optimizations entail physical trade-offs. The measured data reveals two metrics where Service-Aware scores lower than unconstrained Round Robin:

1. Video RTT (20.49 ms in SA vs 11.54 ms in Round Robin):
In Round Robin, the two video flows alternate across servers: per-flow logs show exactly one video stream assigned to srv3 (mean RTT 2.52 ms, 0.0% loss) and one to srv4 (mean RTT 20.56 ms, 1.94% loss), yielding the aggregate average RTT of 11.54 ms. In Service-Aware, both video streams are placed on srv4 (mean RTT 20.49 ms, 1.95% loss). While srv3 has a 1 ms base link delay, it only possesses a 10 Mbit/s total link capacity; concentrating video there risks buffer exhaustion and packet drops. Service-Aware routes video to srv4 (50 Mbit/s bandwidth, 10 ms base delay = 20.49 ms RTT), safely preserving srv3 exclusively for voice traffic.
2. Jain's Fairness Index (0.60 in SA vs 0.72 in Round Robin) & VoIP Latency Split:
Round Robin achieves 0.72 capacity fairness simply because it spreads 8 flows blindly across 4 servers (2 flows each). However, the nominal VoIP mean of 244.40 ± 259.25 ms hides an extreme split across servers: VoIP flows assigned to srv1 suffer 519.3 ms RTT and srv2 suffer 435.2 ms RTT (sharing queues with 100 Mbit/s bulk TCP file transfers), whereas flows on srv3 see 2.51 ms and srv4 see 20.60 ms. In contrast, Service-Aware isolates 100% of VoIP calls to srv3 (2.51 ± 0.07 ms RTT, 0.0% loss), utilizing only 0.25 Mbit/s of srv3's 10 Mbit/s capacity (2.5% utilization). This conscious traffic isolation mechanically depresses the statistical fairness index to 0.60, representing the necessary price of shielding interactive voice calls from bufferbloat.
3. Video Packet Loss Rate (1.95% in SA vs 0.97% in Round Robin):
srv4's link is modeled with a 1.0% physical packet loss rate (link: {loss: 1}). In Round Robin, only one video flow is placed on srv4 (1.94% loss) while the other runs on loss-free srv3 (0.0% loss), resulting in a combined average loss of 0.97%. In Service-Aware, both video streams are routed to srv4, so 100% of video packets traverse this lossy link, resulting in 1.95% measured packet loss. This is a real cost of choosing srv4's 50 Mbit/s bandwidth over srv3's 10 Mbit/s link for dual video streams.
